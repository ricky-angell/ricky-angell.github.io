Net item revenue = price − seller-funded discount. Commission = its actual basis × rate. Fee = its actual basis × rate + fixed fees. Shipping input is the net cost after genuine shipping receipts/subsidies; do not count the same benefit twice. Sales tax is not seller revenue here. Model platform-funded promotions, refund adjustments and other charges from actual settlement evidence rather than treating them as seller discounts.

Reverse price is shown only when both optional bases are blank, meaning both rates scale with net item revenue. Required net price = (other costs + target contribution) ÷ (1 − fee rate − commission rate), rounded up to a cent. Add the fixed seller discount to get the required pre-discount price. Basis overrides disable that shortcut. Maximum affordable commission is a mathematical ceiling under the inputs, not a recommendation or a platform-supported rate.

5. Operations and performance

6. Two complete practice examples

Affiliate: Maya promotes a drawer organizer
  1. Maya checks her real creator eligibility and applies through TikTok Studio. She proceeds with shoppable setup only after approval, records the actual pilot limits and completes required commission-account prompts.
  2. She compares three organizers, confirms the exact size, U.S. fulfillment and seller evidence, and accepts a sample only after reading the posting deadline and content-use terms.
  3. She measures her drawer, tests the sample and films its actual fit. Her script says, “The brand sent this sample, and I may earn a commission through this link.” She shows a limitation and checks the attached product.
  4. She reviews the draft and posts only within her available permissions. She records 1,000 views, 50 product clicks and 5 qualifying purchases: click-through 5%, purchase conversion 10%.
  5. At a hypothetical $30 eligible commission basis and 10% rate, the initial estimate is $15. One full refund before finalization leaves $12 in this simplified example. She distinguishes estimates, final earnings and withdrawal status.
  6. She tests a clearer opening shot next. These invented results teach the workflow; they do not predict earnings.
Seller: Jordan lists a standard stocked product
  1. Jordan uses the correct legal registration type, completes requested verification, tax/payout prompts and account binding, and confirms actual shop status rather than assuming submission is approval.
  2. He verifies category permission, supplier invoices, a physical sample and U.S. stock for the exact variant. The supplier proves a handoff process that can meet the applicable order deadline.
  3. He drafts accurate photos/specifications, SKU mapping, packed weight and return settings. The preview matches the actual item.
  4. He enters $35 revenue, $18 combined non-percentage costs and a hypothetical 6% fee. A 20% affiliate rate leaves $7.90 contribution. His $8 target is missed, so he evaluates a sustainable 15% rate ($9.65 contribution) or a competitive price change before inviting creators.
  5. He declines a seller-funded 20% discount at the original 20% commission: $28 − $18 − $1.68 − $5.60 = $2.72 contribution. He makes no commitment until the actual offer and costs work.
  6. After an eligible real order, he verifies the item and deadline, packs correctly, checks the carrier scan and monitors delivery. He handles after-sales requests through the actual case and reconciles settlement adjustments and bank receipts.
  7. If instead using eligible U.S. POD, he must verify and enable the correct feature/handling time before selling; merely adding “POD” to the title is insufficient.
